# What's Left to Write

We are reading human writing less and less. The answers we used to look for with Google are increasingly being redirected to our preferred AI chatbot. If that's true, should we still put in the effort to write? 

Instead of reading through online blogs, wikipedias, or news articles, I find myself relying on AI summaries. Why read through a whole chapter of a textbook when AI could help summarise the information and tutor you. Clearing email inboxes has never been easier with the help of AI. AI could conceivably do any of the reading or writing tasks that we do.

At this rate, I fear that I might begin to rely on AI summaries of books, movies, or even personal messages instead of reading and watching them myself. Here is where you may draw the line, and I'd agree. But why?

Well, we read and write for many reasons. In school and at work I do so to learn a skill, to get a job done, or to convey a certain message. In these environments no one really cares about how you learn or how you got a task done, as long as the goal is met. That can be to gain a robust understanding of a topic, or become proficient in a skill, or complete a task, the means matter very little.

It is precisely in these areas that AI becomes an invaluable tool. It helps us learn in interactive ways that rival a personal tutor at little to no cost at all. Routine emails or reports with a repetitive layout could be written in a fraction of the time with no loss in quality and accuracy given sufficient human oversight. It can even help you plan your groceries list for meal prep, freeing up time for school or work.

We don't always read and write to learn and convey information. I read books that teach me little to no useful skill or information, but I read them for the same reason I listen to music or hike up a mountain. We don't hike just to reach an end. I can tell you that all my hikes begin and end at the parking lot. We don't listen to music just for the lyrics. Sometimes there are none. I can tell you that Cher's "Believe" is about heartbreak and learning to live without someone you love (I only figured that out about 10 years after I first heard it. Yes, that is ridiculous. In my defence English isn't my first language.), but that isn't why it's popular.

To put it plainly, often we read not because of what was written, but how it was written. Skilled authors could have their words engage in a delightful dance immersing the readers in its rhythm. Leaving readers bathing is prose that could make the seemingly ordinary seem extraordinary.

We also read to connect with other people in one way or another. It could be a blog that you follow and you deeply value not just the people they spend time with, the places they have been, and the things they own, but their perspective and how they view the world, the people, and things around them. Their perspective influences what we pay attention to in our lives as well. We truly see what we look for. 

AI is undeniably gaining dominance in fields that were traditionally the domain of human writers, but I believe there are some areas where AI cannot replace humans completely. AI will always be able to assist, but spaces where genuine personal experiences are valued above all will require a human touch. For me, those are reviews I weigh, memoirs I read, and letters I receive. AI may be able to mimic a human very well, but authenticity is hard to replicate. Reading, like writing, is one of the ways we connect with another person, and that connection only means something when a real person is on the other end.

Personally I have always liked the idea of starting a blog, but found a reason not to every time. Especially in today's rapidly changing landscape, it is harder to find a place online for our writing. It is not easy to hit a moving target. So this blog in a lot of ways is a personal exploration to find reasons for myself to begin and write. I will miss 100% of the shots I don't take.

Even if no one is reading, there is still a compelling reason to write. It is perhaps the same reason why people journal. Writing helps us figure out what we are thinking. Helps us organise and formulize our thoughts and ideas better. To lay them out in front of us in scattered pieces so we can better bring them together to create a whole.

Not every written piece will be from a human, and it will always be that way, but written pieces are ultimately for people. Only you can truly know what you like, and no one else can write it for you. So don't just write what you think others might want to read. Write first what you'd want to read yourself. That is what's left to write.
